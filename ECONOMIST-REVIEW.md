# Economist review checklist (v5.9)

> **Note (Nov 28, 2026).** The results repository named in this document, `BetterToBest/compassionism-sim-results`, is offline: GitHub disabled it. Every figure can be reproduced from the engine commit named beside it, with the commands in `REPRODUCE.md`. The results folders are deposited in an archive with a permanent identifier, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24615619 (copied on OSF at osf.io/7v5ur); folders not in the archive are listed in `data/results-manifest.json`.

One page for an independent economist, rebuilt for v5.9 on Nov 17, 2026 (engine `104691f`). Each row names one thing to check, where it is in the repository, and what the model does today. Mark each row agree, disagree or cannot tell, with a line of why. You need not do every row. Rows about what the framework means (as opposed to how it is modelled) belong to its author, Duke Johnson; say so in a comment. The model in one page: [model-card.html](https://bettertobest.github.io/compassionism-simulation/model-card.html); in full: [ODD.md](ODD.md) and [MODEL_SPEC.md](MODEL_SPEC.md).

Send it by opening an issue with the "Economist review" template (Issues, New issue), or by a comment on the pull request or discussion where you found the model.

## First: the four assumptions the v5.9 results rest on

| # | Check | Where | What the model does today | Agree / disagree / cannot tell, and why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Full backing.** The main row assumes every dollar the programme pays is matched by new output | TESTBED_SPEC.md section 0; MODEL_SPEC.md 14.0; `dev/reports/s64-round2-q4-backing.md` | This is the design's statement, a design parameter, not a model result: the model's own mechanism for new output reaches 0.2 to 1.9% of the payout. Inflation stays under 5% a year only above about 86 to 90% backing; at the mechanism's own point it runs at 15 to 53 points a year. The backing curve (0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1) is published beside the main row. Is leading with full backing, with the curve beside it, a fair presentation? |  |
| 2 | **No limit on supply below full backing.** Demand never runs into what the economy can produce | `dev/reports/s64-round2-q4-backing.md`, item 4 and its note of Nov 17, 2026; MODEL_SPEC 6.12, 14.3 | The poverty effects barely depend on backing because, with no new output, the real goods recipients gain are paid for only by higher prices on everyone, and wages, the BU and the basket all follow those prices. A rationing reading is planned for the next engine version. Which limit on real supply would you add first, and how would you size it? |  |
| 3 | **Wages follow prices in full** (`wIdx` 1) | MODEL_SPEC 6.1, 6.12; `dev/reports/s70-screen.md`, item 3 | Wages are indexed to the price level each year. At no backing this is the largest mover of programme inflation (μ* 20 to 34 points a year). Partial indexation is planned as a reading. What share is defensible for a programme of this size, and over what lag? |  |
| 4 | **Asset returns under high inflation** | MODEL_SPEC 14.6, 14.7; `dev/reports/s59-asset-classes.md`, `s71-creditor-side.md` | Real assets keep their real value (plus Shiller's real gains), nominal assets earn 0.46 of inflation, floating debt reprices in full and fixed-rate debt is eroded by inflation (debt relief). At full backing the erosion is under 3% of any wealth gain; at no backing, how far real assets follow inflation is the largest mover of wealth poverty and median wealth. Is that portfolio behaviour plausible at inflation of 15-50% a year, and who should bear the creditors' loss (five readings are run)? |  |
