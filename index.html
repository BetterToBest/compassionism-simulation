CCO — Creative Currency Octaves

Dual-currency layer. Participants get monthly Basic Units (BU), redeemable at PTF businesses, converted to primary currency at merit-scaled rates.

BU — Basic Units

The community currency issued by CCO. Pegged 1:1 to primary currency, redeemable below market price at PTF establishments, expires monthly.

PTF — Public Trust Foundations

Community-owned, not-for-profit businesses that accept BU. Modelled as a cost-reduction mechanism (12–16% lower costs), not a wealth-addition channel.

PTH — Public Trust Housing

Community-owned housing; monthly payments build Acre Equity instead of paying market rent or mortgage interest.

AE — Acre Equity

Community equity built by PTH residents, appreciating ~4%/yr, accessible at exit subject to a tenure-based liquidity haircut.

SZH — Social Zone Harmonization

Spatial governance layer organizing where PTF/PTH deploy; gates cooperative synergy effects behind a zone-participation threshold.

CIP — Citizens Internet Portal

Digital platform administering CCO currency operations and democratic voting for PTH/PTF decisions.

BLEI — Basic Living Economic Index

Primary welfare metric: how many days of basic living an agent's accessible resources cover (cash + income + benefit buffers).

EDC — Extractive Drain Coefficient

Share of income captured by contracts that build zero household wealth (rent, interest, fees). PTH payments have EDC = 0.

FBS — Financial Bandwidth Score

Monthly residual resources above the basic-living threshold; gates an agent's probability of advancing an octave level.

What each measure asks, and how it is counted

Why five measures. They answer different questions. The stock measures ask whether people have a cushion; the flow measures ask whether this year's income covers this year's needs; extreme poverty asks whether people are homeless. A policy can move one and not another, so each is reported against where people started (year 0) as well as against the Baseline.

Stock: what people own at the end of the run. Wealth poverty counts net wealth below POVERTY_LINE ($25,000). BLEI poverty counts people whose savings, income and BU cover fewer than 30 days of basic living costs. BLEI counts BU and cost relief from the day someone enrols, so its year-0 figure is shown under Baseline rules, with the figure under your settings' rules beneath it.

Flow: what people earn in the final year. Cash income is wage income plus CCO conversion proceeds; PTH appreciation is left out, as a capital gain. Relative income poverty counts cash income below 60% of that scenario's own median, so when most incomes rise together it can stay flat or even rise. Basket poverty compares income with the cost of a living-wage basket (LIVING_WAGE_ANNUAL, adjusted for inflation): "net" uses the prices each person actually pays after CCO, PTF and PTH cost relief; "gross" uses full prices. At year 0 the median person's wage income ($39,945) is below the basket ($49,370), so about two-thirds of people start in basket poverty. That starting gap is what drives the Baseline's decline (Known Limitations; CONTRIBUTING.md v4.17).

Extreme: homeless, with necessities from charity if at all. The engine models no housing, mental illness or vocation, and at the US rate (about 22 per 10,000 people, HUD's 2025 point-in-time count) this is about one person in a 500-agent run. So it is an estimated share built from three pathways, and these rows read per 10,000 people. Economic (73% at year 0) moves with the share of people in housing distress, whose income plus savings cannot cover this year's basket; CCO income and PTF and PTH cost relief lower it. Serious mental illness (25%) does not respond to income, so CCO alone leaves it unchanged; wellness zones, which need both PTH and SZH, lower it by up to half (Housing First trial evidence), scaled by zone coherence. Voluntary (2%) covers ascetics, itinerant monastics and renunciants; it is the same in every scenario, and the share will be updated as data sources become available. This definition differs from the World Bank's, which counts income below $3.00 a day. See Known Limitations and CONTRIBUTING.md v4.18.

Shock Response automatic BU increase in recessions, and what it offsets (v4.19)
How much of a recession's rise in housing distress an automatic BU increase offsets
How this is measured, and what it cannot show

Measure. Housing distress is the v4.18 measure: this year's cash income plus savings cannot cover this year's basket. For each seed the study runs your settings with recessions off and with recessions on under each rule, on the same agents, recession path and random draws. The excess is the difference in recession years, averaged over every recession year of every seed, for CCO participants and non-participants separately. Excess extreme poverty converts the all-agent excess through the v4.18 overlay's economic pathway. Extra BU is the added allocation as a share of the base allocation over the run.

Shock-neutral. The multiplier at which participants' excess distress in recession years is zero, found by false position from ×1 and ×3 (widened to ×4 if needed). At the reference settings it is ×1.36 over 300 seeds (node harness.js stabilizer), shown as ×1.35 on the slider; the scaled rule's equivalent is about +2.8% BU per 1% income loss. These are the sliders' defaults. The Research Hub's ×1.20 offsets about half of the rise.

Why the default is a target, not the peak of a curve. Returns to a larger increase are close to linear, because the engine has no price response to BU issuance and no budget constraint. A larger increase never looks worse, and the model cannot test whether it would be inflationary. The default is the smallest increase that fully offsets a recession for the people it reaches.

How the BU amount reaches a household (v4.19). Through v4.18, CCO's cost relief was a flat 20% of the basket whatever the BU amount, so a larger BU helped only through conversion proceeds, which runYear() credits once a year. The framework describes BU as redeemable for essential goods, so from v4.19 the relief scales with the BU actually issued: 20% at the $1,200 reference, in proportion above and below it, up to a 50% ceiling (a placeholder). Every $1,200 preset is unchanged. Under the v4.18 rule the shock-neutral multiplier was ×2.35.

Reach. A BU increase reaches only CCO participants. Emergency enrollment extends the CCO cost relief to non-participants in triggered years; its 50% take-up default is a placeholder with no source.

Place-based BU premiums (not simulated)

The same lever can target places as well as times. A region that wants to attract residents, such as a cold-climate area with little winter sunlight, could add a BU premium for residents who meet a residency requirement, much as Alaska's Permanent Fund Dividend pays each qualifying resident after a full calendar year of residence. Whether such payments move people is not well established: a 2019 University of Alaska review lists the dividend's effect on in-migration among the questions not yet studied. The closest measured case, Tulsa Remote, pays remote workers $10,000 to relocate; an Upjohn Institute evaluation (Bartik, 2025) estimates that 58–70% of participants would not have moved without it. Remote workers are a different population from households in poverty, and the same evaluation notes that added housing supply is needed to realise the benefits. A payment tied to a place can also be absorbed by higher local rents; Public Trust Housing, whose costs are set outside the private rental market, could limit that. This simulation has no geography, so it reports no result for this use.

▶ 📋 Assumptions, ODD Protocol & Known Limitations v4.19 — click to expand 📐 ODD Protocol — Grimm et al. (2010) standard ABM description
Overview: Purpose & Entities

Purpose: Explore Compassionism parameter space; identify conditions under which poverty targets are met; provide comparable scenario analyses for policy discussion. Not a forecasting model. Entities: Households (agents) characterised by economic state. Scales: 100–2000 agents; 5–30 annual time steps. Each agent represents one adult household unit.

Overview: State Variables

Per agent: wealth (USD), wage (SIU), octave (skill tier 0–maxOct), quality (1–9×), automationRisk — 47%/53% Beta(6,1)/Beta(1,6) bimodal mixture (v4.12 fix: this line said "∈ [0.2,1.0]" — the retired pre-v4.3 uniform draw's exact bounds — while the Initialization card two rows down already correctly described the current bimodal mixture; an external audit caught the two cards contradicting each other within this same panel), inCCO/inPTF/inPTH (participation flags), buBalance, acreEquity. Aggregate observables: poverty rate, Gini, BLEI tier distribution, EDC, stability index.

Overview: Process Overview & Scheduling

Time step: one year. simulate() runs the Traditional Welfare Baseline's full trajectory, then CCO Only's, then Your Settings year by year — each on its own copy of the same latent population and its own copy of the same seeded stream (common random numbers). Within one runYear() call: A. Once per year, before any agent moves — (1) PTH appreciation bonus from SZH coherence; (2) the SZH synergy coefficient θ and the two terms it gates (PTF conversion bonus, SZH→PTF induction probability); (3) a snapshot of realised PTF membership; (4) realised PTH membership; (5) population-wide inflation damping, PTF then PTH, each scaled by its realised share; (6) dollarCost, the BASE_DAILY_COST-based figure FBS and PTH sizing use; (7) the recession income multiplier; (8) the live PTF count, only if the adoption cap is on; (9) the AI displacement rate; (10) the automatic-stabilizer terms (v4.19, inert unless switched on): the recession trigger, the effective BU allocation, and the emergency-enrollment line. B. Then each agent in turn, in array order — (1) NaN rescue, then a record of start-of-year wealth for the extreme-poverty overlay (v4.18; reporting only, read by nothing in the loop); (2) exactly eight RNG draws in a fixed order (wage variance, BU spend fraction, CIP quality bump, octave advancement, SZH→PTF induction, SZH→PTF share, PTH appreciation noise, PTF adoption), whatever the agent's status — the v4.3 common-random-numbers guarantee, machine-checked by the validation suite's invariants check since v4.16; (3) BLEI on the agent's start-of-year state; (4) wage growth, floored at 80% of last year's wage; (5) cost-reduction factor from start-of-year PTF/PTH/CCO membership; (6) wealth += wage income × incomeShock − inflation-adjusted LIVING_WAGE_ANNUAL × cost factor; (7) CCO participants only: BU balance decay plus the new allocation (capped at 3× monthly BU), spend, CIP quality bump, conversion at the octave/quality/Phi/PTF-capped rate less progressive tax (× incomeShock), then FBS-gated octave advancement using the new wage, then SZH→PTF induction; (8) PTH members: tenure +1, payment→equity contribution, appreciation; (9) agents not yet in PTF, from the second year on: innovation + distress + Bass adoption check; (10) wealth floor clamp. Timing consequences: an agent who joins PTF in a given year gets its cost relief from the next year; adoptions within a year do not change that year's Bass imitation term (a start-of-year snapshot) but do count against the optional cap immediately; FBS compares this year's grown wage with a cost factor set by start-of-year membership; and agents never read one another's state except through the two start-of-year membership counts and the live cap counter — so array order matters only when the PTF cap is on. (New v4.16 — the ODD standard's third element, previously missing from this panel. Enumerated line by line against runYear() as CONTRIBUTING.md Good First Issues item (e) asked, rather than transcribed from an audit's attempt.)

Design Concepts: Emergence & Adaptation

Emergence: Poverty, inequality, and BLEI tier distributions emerge from agent-level rules — not directly imposed. Adaptation: Octave advancement gated by FBS (Mullainathan & Shafir bandwidth constraint). PTF adoption via economic distress and social diffusion (Bass 1969 model). No explicit utility maximisation — behavioural rules only. Feedback structure (v4.14, named explicitly per an external audit's runYear()-level review): runYear() is not a set of independent annual adjustments — several mechanisms couple into genuine reinforcing loops. Welfare (BLEI, computed from the PRIOR year's state) drives this year's wage-growth bonus; the resulting wage feeds FBS; FBS gates octave advancement; and octave itself then feeds BACK into wage growth (WAGE_OCTAVE_BONUS) and raises the CCO conversion-rate ceiling for the following year — so two otherwise-identical agents who diverge in octave once can diverge further in both wage and conversion capacity thereafter. A parallel loop runs through housing: PTH lowers annual cost, which raises FBS, which raises advancement probability, which raises wages — making PTH a liquidity-and-mobility mechanism as well as a housing subsidy. Neither loop is a bug; both are the intended behavioural design. The practical implication is that a reported "CCO effect" or "PTH effect" is a compound effect (currency/cost relief + financial bandwidth + skill advancement + wage growth + conversion compounding), not a single isolated channel — worth keeping in mind when reading any headline delta, and a candidate for formal pathway decomposition in a future release (see CONTRIBUTING.md's Model Architecture Feedback).

Design Concepts: Interaction & Stochasticity

Interaction: Indirect market-mediated only — no direct agent-to-agent interaction. Cooperative effects via PTF/SZH zone parameters. Stochasticity: Seeded PRNG (mulberry32); wage variance ±10%; recession events 10% annual probability (beta(5,2) severity); BU spend rates 60–90%; octave advancement (FBS-gated probability). Same seed + params → identical run.

Details: Initialization

Agent wealth: lognormal(CFG.WEALTH_INIT_MU,CFG.WEALTH_INIT_SIGMA) → median ~$36K ⓘ. Wage: lognormal(3.5,0.5) → median ~33 SIU. Octave: beta(2,5)×maxOct. Quality: lognormal(log(maxMult×0.5),0.4). automationRisk: 47%/53% bimodal mixture, Beta(6,1) high-risk / Beta(1,6) low-risk (v4.3; was uniform[0.2,1.0] through v4.2 — see Cumulative Bug Fixes). λ (FBS capability coefficient, v4.0): uniform[0.0001654,0.0013233] per agent (v4.4: rescaled ÷6.0456 from the pre-v4.4 [0.001,0.008] — see Cumulative Bug Fixes). Participation flags assigned at initialization probabilities. Baseline, CCO-only, and Main are instantiated from one shared canonical latent population (v4.2) — same n individuals, three policy configs — with no initial-wealth adjustment applied to any of them as of v4.4 (previously baseline agents received an undocumented-provenance ×0.85 wealth haircut; removed — see Cumulative Bug Fixes).

Details: Submodels

Annual update (runYear): the full verified order — the once-per-year population terms, the fixed eight-draw RNG schedule, and the SZH→PTF induction step this summary used to omit — is now in "Overview: Process Overview & Scheduling," above (v4.16). In brief: wage growth with diminishing returns → living cost (LIVING_WAGE_ANNUAL, inflation-adjusted, × the agent's cost-reduction factor) → CCO conversion at an octave-capped rate → FBS-gated octave advancement → SZH→PTF induction → PTH equity contribution and appreciation → PTF adoption (innovation + distress + Bass imitation) → wealth floor clamp. BLEI = (liquid + γ·wage·WAGE_TO_USD + buFood) / dailyCost (v4.0: wage converted to USD before summing; v4.4: onto the same real-dollar anchor the main loop uses — see Empirical Calibration below). Gini (v4.0): computed on EDC-adjusted net wealth, W_nominal − EDC×Y×12. Recession: beta(5,2) incomeMultiplier ∈ [0.70,0.95], NBER-calibrated (nber.org) — mode 0.90 (10% loss), mean ≈0.88 (~12% loss). Population-wide inflation is damped by PTF and PTH presence, each scaled by that system's REALISED population share (PTF since v4.14, PTH since v4.15 — previously the initial PTF slider and a flat PTH toggle factor respectively; see Cumulative Bug Fixes). Automatic stabilizers, and CCO cost relief that scales with BU (v4.19, requested by Duke)

Implements the Research Hub's crisis protocols (Integrated Implementation Roadmap, Appendix G) as opt-in levers in a new Automatic Stabilizers panel: a BU increase in recession years (fixed multiplier, or scaled to the recession's income loss) above a minimum-loss trigger; suspended BU expiry and emergency enrollment of non-participants while triggered; and BU indexed to inflation above a trigger. Each works through a per-year effective BU amount inside runYear() and draws no random number, so runs with and without them stay common-random-number paired. All are off by default, so the seed-42 regression is unchanged. A new Shock Response card reports this run's tally and, on demand, a 30-seed paired study that measures what a rule offsets and finds the shock-neutral multiplier for your settings. CCO's cost relief now scales with the BU issued (Duke's decision; see Known Limitations), which leaves every $1,200 preset unchanged; the calibrated defaults are ×1.35, or +2.8% BU per 1% income loss. The Poverty by Five Measures card was also reworded for clarity. A reader note on place-based BU premiums, which the engine cannot simulate, sits in the Shock Response card.

FBS_LAMBDA recalibrated — a behavioral choice, not a dimensionally-forced one (v4.4; correction added post-release)

The FBS gate's λ coefficient has units of 1/USD by its own original comment — it multiplies a dollar-denominated surplus (fbs) to produce an advancement probability. Once fbs's dominant input (Yusd, above) grew ~6× under the wage-scale fix, λ's effective scale was left uncorrected, pushing pAdvance toward saturation (≈1) for nearly all agents regardless of BU level — measurably weakening the BU-monotonicity regression check (one seed dropped from a clean win to an exact tie at n=200). FBS_LAMBDA_LO/HI are rescaled ÷6.0456 (the precise WAGE_TO_USD/legacy-SIU_TO_USD ratio) from 0.001/0.008 to 0.0001654/0.0013233, preserving the HI/LO=8× heterogeneity shape. This entry originally called that rescale "dimensionally-necessary" — that overstated it. fbs mixes the wage-derived term that changed scale with fixed-dollar terms (BU face value, basic cost) that didn't, so fbs itself doesn't scale by one clean factor; the rescale is a chosen behavioral recalibration to restore useful advancement-probability variation, kept because the un-rescaled range — matching the BLEI paper's own published λ — already saturates (89.8–100% advancement probability) at the paper's own worked example, leaving little of λ's intended role as an individual-capability differentiator. All six VAL_TESTS pass 5/5 post-change — internal consistency, not empirical validation of these specific values. Still spec-derived, not independently sourced; open calibration status documented in CONTRIBUTING.md and the BLEI paper's own Index IV note.

gamma(1) silently returned a constant, not a random draw (found during v4.4 harness work, unrelated to either audit)

The general-purpose gamma(a) rejection sampler divides by (a−1) inside a log term; at a=1 exactly this is x/0, making the acceptance test 0×log(∞)=NaN and therefore always false. Every call exhausted all 5,000 rejection iterations and fell through to return a — gamma(1) returned the literal constant 1 on every single call (confirmed: 20/20 identical samples in testing), not a random Exponential(1) draw. This directly narrows beta(6,1)/beta(1,6) — drawAutomationRisk()'s high/low-risk components, used once per agent on every simulation run — and wasted the full 5,000 dead iterations per call in the process (≈2.7s of pure waste per 500-agent run, browser or harness). Fixed with the exact closed-form solution: Gamma(shape=1,scale=1) is exactly Exponential(1), so -ln(RNG()) replaces the rejection loop entirely for this case — standard, not a new formula. Population construction is now roughly 200× faster as a side effect, which is what made this release's N=5,000 study practical to run. Verified against all six VAL_TESTS post-fix.

📊 Empirical Calibration & Mechanism Documentation
Daily vs. simulation cost scales — what v4.0 fixed, what v4.3 fixed, and what's a historical constant today (rewritten v4.12 — see note at the end)

BASE_DAILY_COST ($68.33/day, BLS CES 2023), SIM_COST_SCALE ($1,500, a calibrated ABM scale unit — NOT a dollar figure), WAGE_TO_USD (≈100.52, Census/BLS-sourced), and LIVING_WAGE_ANNUAL ($49,370) are four different anchors introduced at different points in this project's history, and it's easy to lose track of which one drives which formula today. An external review (Refine.ink, Aug 2026) originally flagged that wage (SIU) was being added directly into USD-typed sums in several places. Fixed in v4.0: agentBLEI, calcBLEIComponents, the FBS advancement gate, and the Gini net-wealth adjustment converted wage to USD via a derived constant, SIU_TO_USD = (BASE_DAILY_COST×365)/SIM_COST_SCALE ≈ 16.6 — these formulas don't compound over time, so a conversion constant there was safe. Deliberately NOT changed at v4.0: the main wealth-accumulation loop — an early attempt at a full fix proved dangerously sensitive to the conversion constant, since wage compounds against cost over up to 30 years (a ~3.5× range of candidate values swung 20-year outcomes from universal wealth-floor collapse to implausible multi-million-dollar median wealth). Fixed in v4.3: a validated anchor was found — WAGE_TO_USD (wage side) and LIVING_WAGE_ANNUAL (cost side), both passing all six VAL_TESTS including BU-monotonicity — and the main loop was rebuilt on it (see the wage/cost unit-fix entry further down this same panel for the full redistribution finding that came with it: this was not a "just bigger numbers" fix). Fixed in v4.4: BLEI/FBS/Gini's own wage-side conversion moved off SIU_TO_USD and onto the same WAGE_TO_USD anchor the main loop now uses, closing a ~6× gap between what an agent's wage was "worth" depending which formula asked. SIU_TO_USD is retired as of v4.4 and is not read by any function in this file today. SIM_COST_SCALE is likewise no longer load-bearing for wage conversion or the main loop; it survives in CFG purely as a historical constant, kept rather than deleted so old exported CSV/JSON files and this changelog's own worked examples from prior versions stay interpretable. Standard ABM calibrated-unit convention (Epstein & Axtell, 1996); base daily cost sourced from BLS CES 2023 (bls.gov/cex). BU wealth creation — counterparty accounting

BU → quality conversion does not create wealth ex nihilo. PTF businesses accept BUs and provide real goods/services (food, utilities) at cooperative prices. Quality multiplier reflects productivity gains distributed to workers via cooperative ownership (Mondragon model, Whyte & Whyte 1991). Conversion economy circulates within the PTF network. v4.0: external review correctly noted the engine credited conversion proceeds without debiting any treasury/production account. As a first step, cumulative conversion proceeds are now tracked and surfaced in CSV/JSON export (Conversion Ledger) for transparency. A genuine aggregate production constraint — one that could ration agent-level conversion when exhausted — is a further economic-modelling design decision (production function, capacity limits, failure behaviour) that isn't implemented yet; see Known Limitations.

PTH Acre Equity — payments now build equity (v4.0; sub-claims corrected v4.15 and v4.16)

THREE separate PTH benefits as of v4.0: (1) housing cost reduction — a dimensionless 0.65 factor (cf*=0.65) applied to the main loop's LIVING_WAGE_ANNUAL-based cost, not SIM_COST_SCALE, which no function in this file reads (v4.15 fix: this card previously said "35% of SIM_COST_SCALE" — accurate at v4.0, superseded by v4.3's wealth-loop unit fix, and missed by the v4.12 staleness sweep that corrected the equivalent claim elsewhere in this file; caught by an external audit) — (2) a share (25%) of that cost saving routed into Acre Equity — the "payments build equity" mechanism the docs described but the engine previously didn't implement (it was appreciation-only) — and (3) Acre Equity appreciation, where a tenure-cohort share of the appreciation (15% at tenure=1, ramping to 85% at tenure≥5 — see pthLiquidShare() and the tenure-cohort haircut entry, above) is credited to liquid personal wealth, while the full appreciation is also added to acreEquity (illiquid) (v4.16 correction: the v4.15 rewrite of this card said the non-liquid remainder goes to acreEquity. Framework design intent — macro constraints

The framework is explicitly designed to approach universal prosperity for the coming automation wave, with BU as treasury-backed community infrastructure. Absence of housing scarcity and fiscal limits is a stated hypothesis — that cooperative institutions structurally reduce these frictions — not a modeling oversight. This is a documented divergence from traditional macro models.

⚠ Known Limitations
No demographic structure

Agents lack age, retirement, disability, household size, or mortality. Acceptable for structural scenario comparison; not suitable for policy forecasting requiring life-cycle dynamics. Future: age cohorts and household transitions (see CONTRIBUTING.md).

No direct agent-to-agent interaction

All social effects mediated by aggregate zone parameters (SZH) rather than explicit networks. A small-world network structure would better model PTF zone clustering and SZH coherence. Noted as a future development direction in CONTRIBUTING.md.

"Traditional Welfare Baseline" is a no-intervention counterfactual, not a calibrated model of the actual U.S. safety net (new clarification, v4.12)

An external audit flagged that the baseline's name and its "real-world reference point" framing (now softened, see the preset tooltip) can read as a claim this scenario reproduces actual U.S. welfare institutions. It doesn't: pBase sets every architecture off and applies a flat 3% CPI, with no SNAP/EITC/TANF/SSI-SSDI benefit receipt, Medicaid, housing assistance, or unemployment insurance modelled as income to any agent. (The Social Security-Anchored Cohorts table tags agents by wage relative to real 2026 SSI/SSDI/retirement benefit levels for comparison purposes — it does not credit those benefits to baseline agents as income.) This scenario is best read as "what happens with none of these five architectures and no other offsetting mechanism," a deliberately clean no-intervention counterfactual — not as a calibrated stand-in for the current U.S. policy mix, which a rigorous comparison would need to model separately. Adding a genuinely calibrated current-policy benchmark alongside this counterfactual is a real, substantive future scenario, not attempted here; see CONTRIBUTING.md's Model Architecture Feedback.

The Baseline comparison runs at a different inflation rate than Your Settings unless you match it (new disclosure and opt-in, v4.16)

Since v4.0 the Baseline comparison has been anchored to a fixed 3% CPI (CFG.BASELINE_CPI_RATE), while Your Settings and CCO Only use the inflation slider — 0% in Full Integration, CCO Only and High AI. Every vs-Baseline figure in those presets therefore mixes the policy effect with a 3-point inflation difference. The replication page motivates the 0% default as the framework's price-stability hypothesis for cooperative essential-goods provision; as implemented, that hypothesis is an assumed input rather than a model output — at 0% the engine's own PTF/PTH inflation damping has nothing to act on, and CCO Only, which has neither PTF nor PTH, runs at 0% too — so its assumed effect is counted inside the policy effect. Measured with harness.js infl-match (seeds 1–500, 500 agents, 20 years, shocks off): Baseline wealth poverty is 71.8% at 3% but 50.5% at 0%; its median wealth is pinned at WEALTH_FLOOR in 100% of runs at 3% but 0.6% at 0%; Full Integration's wealth poverty is 15.3% at 0% but 28.8% at 3%. So of the unmatched 56.5-point poverty gap, 35.2 points remain when both run at 0% and 43.0 when both run at 3% — about 24–38% of the headline gap is inflation, not policy. The long-documented finding that Baseline's median is "pinned at the floor in 100% of runs" is a product of this mismatch, not of the Baseline's policy mix alone. The new Match Baseline inflation to my settings toggle (Economic Environment) runs the Baseline at your slider's rate, isolating the policy effect; it changes the Baseline comparison only, never Your Settings' own trajectory, and is off by default so every documented figure is unchanged. A warning appears whenever the two rates differ and the toggle is off. Which behaviour should be the default is a framework-level decision logged for Duke in CONTRIBUTING.md, not settled here.

The papers' headline figures (98% poverty reduction, $82,000 median wealth) are not produced by this engine (new disclosure, v4.17)

Independent NEEC maintainers found this and it is confirmed here (seeds 1–500, 500 agents, 20 years). Full Integration reduces wealth poverty by 78.7% and BLEI poverty by 82.2% against the shipped Baseline comparison. Those figures shrink under a fairer comparator: 69.7% / 74.4% with inflation matched at 0%, 59.9% / 63.4% with both scenarios at 3%. Against year 0 wealth poverty falls 59.5% (37.8% → 15.3%), while BLEI poverty rises (10.3% → 12.6%). No comparator gives 98%. Median wealth is $526,629, not $82,000; TARGET_WEALTH, TARGET_POVERTY and TARGET_GINI are paper targets carried as constants, not model outputs. Reconciling the papers with the engine is a decision for the framework's author; the figures are regenerated by node harness.js headline.

Most agents start below the living-wage basket, which is why the Baseline deteriorates (new disclosure, v4.17)

The median year-0 wage income is $39,945 (exp(3.5) × 12 × WAGE_TO_USD) against a LIVING_WAGE_ANNUAL basket of $49,370, so about two-thirds of agents (66.6%) start in basket poverty. The Baseline has no transfers, so those agents run an annual deficit from year 1 and draw down their wealth; at 3% CPI, costs also outgrow wages (about 1–1.8%/yr). At 0% inflation the Baseline's wealth poverty plateaus near 50% and its basket poverty falls from 66.6% to 47.0%; at 3% wealth poverty keeps rising to 71.8% and basket poverty to 82.1%. The same gap produces a transient under Full Integration: BLEI poverty rises from year 0 to about 20% at year 5 before falling to 12.6%, while basket poverty falls steadily (66.6% → 40.6% at year 1 → 10.0%), because initial wealth is drawn independently of wage and deficit agents run down that stock. About 7.7 points of the apparent rise is measurement: at year 0, BLEI already credits participants' BU and cost relief (2.6% under Full Integration rules vs 10.3% for the same agents under Baseline rules). The Poverty by Four Measures card reports both year-0 figures and change against year 0 as well as against the Baseline. Whether to recalibrate the wage distribution or the cost anchor is a decision for the framework's author.

Extreme poverty is an overlay on engine state, not an engine output (new, v4.18)

The engine models no housing tenure, mental illness or vocation, and at US scale (about 22 per 10,000) the measure is about one agent in a 500-agent run. It is therefore an expected share. Year 0 equals HUD's 2025 point-in-time rate by construction. The economic pathway (73%) moves with the share of agents whose income plus savings cannot cover the year's living-wage basket; the serious-mental-illness pathway (25%) changes only where PTH and SZH together provide wellness zones, by up to 50% scaled by zone coherence; the voluntary pathway (2%) is the same in every scenario. Three inputs are assumptions adopted by the framework's author and open to revision as data sources become available: the 2% voluntary share, the proportionality of economic homelessness to housing distress, and an SMI pathway that does not worsen with the economy. Under CCO Only the measure ends near its year-0 level: participants' housing distress falls, while non-participants inherit the Baseline's deterioration (see the living-wage basket entry). This definition differs from the World Bank's "extreme poverty," which counts income below $3.00 a day.

CCO cost relief now scales with BU; conversion is still credited once a year (changed, v4.19)

Through v4.18, CCO's cost relief was a flat 20% of the living-wage basket whenever BU was above zero, so the BU amount reached a household only through conversion proceeds. From v4.19 the relief scales with the BU issued: 20% at the $1,200 reference, proportionally above and below it, capped at 50% (CCO_RELIEF_CAP, a placeholder with no source; it binds from $3,000 of effective BU). Every $1,200 preset, including the seed-42 regression, is unchanged; Stress Test ($900) and any run at another BU amount move. Conversion proceeds are still credited once per simulated year, although wages are annualised ×12; crediting twelve allocations a year was measured and not adopted, because it would scale the unconstrained conversion channel (see the stock-flow entry) twelvefold and move seed 42 to 9.2% wealth poverty and $1.19M median wealth. The 20% at $1,200 is carried over unchanged from earlier releases.

Prices are exogenous (named explicitly, v4.17)

Inflation is a fixed input rate, damped only by realised PTF and PTH membership; no price responds to demand, BU issuance, conversion volume, recession or automation. Recession scales income only, and automation slows wage growth only. An endogenous price channel would be needed before the model could test claims that the framework is non-inflationary, rather than assume them.

Two thresholds from the papers are proxies or labels, not dynamics (corrected, v4.17)

55% CCO participation: the papers' minimum-viable level appears here only as a run warning. No line of runYear() reads aggregate participation, and a participation sweep shows no break at 55% (wealth poverty 23.2% at 45%, 20.9% at 55%, 18.4% at 65%). SZH synergy θ: the paper gates θ on PTF merchant density; the code gates it on the SZH zone-coherence slider, and realised PTF density never enters. Building either as a dynamic would change the model and is logged as a decision.

"Recession" and "AI automation" are narrower than their names suggest (named explicitly, v4.14)

Both mechanisms are real and calibrated, but each implements a reduced-form slice of what its name evokes, not the full phenomenon — worth stating plainly rather than leaving a reader to infer the scope. Recession scales two income flows and nothing else: earned wage income and CCO conversion proceeds, both multiplied by incomeShock (which also carries every agent's ordinary ±10% annual wage-variance draw, recession or not) (v4.16 correction: this entry said recession "only ever multiplies earned wage income" — convGain is multiplied by the same incomeShock). It does not touch asset prices, employment status, housing costs, business activity, government transfers, or interest rates. "Exogenous labor-income shock" is a more precise description than "recession model." AI automation subtracts a displacement term directly from wage GROWTH (popAIDisp × automationRisk) — there is no explicit job-loss event, job search, unemployment spell, or re-employment transition; an agent's income slows, it never actually disappears and returns. Both are legitimate, common first-pass ABM simplifications (and each is already flagged individually elsewhere in this panel — see "No demographic structure" and the wage-floor entry, further up), but the specific claim "this is a recession/automation model" is easy to over-read as more general than either mechanism actually is. A fuller macro-shock model (asset prices, employment transitions, transfers) and an occupation-based re-employment model for AI are both real future-work candidates — see CONTRIBUTING.md's Model Architecture Feedback.

automationRisk distribution — resolved in v4.3 (flagged v3.4)

Through v4.2, automationRisk ∈ [0.2, 1.0] was drawn from a uniform distribution — real automation exposure is highly bimodal and occupation-dependent (Frey & Osborne, 2013; Autor, 2015), and the uniform assumption understated polarization in High Automation scenarios. As of v4.3, it's a 47%/53% mixture (Beta(6,1) high-risk / Beta(1,6) low-risk) matching that literature's occupational split — see Cumulative Bug Fixes, above. Occupation-stratified (not just bimodal) risk remains a further-refinement item in CONTRIBUTING.md.

System Stability needs at least 8 simulated years to mean anything (found and partially fixed, v4.13)

The metric is the inverse coefficient of variation over the run's final quarter. At the slider's 5-year minimum that quarter is barely two years, and before v4.13 it was rounded down to a single point, which made the score a constant 0.99 for every 5–7 year run (see Cumulative Bug Fixes). The window floor is now 2, which is enough to compute a real variance but is still a very small sample: treat System Stability on any run shorter than ~8–10 years as indicative at best, and prefer a 20-year run (the reference configuration) when the number matters. This is a limitation of the window, on top of the separate, already-documented limitation of the CV formula itself (below), which cannot tell a thriving equilibrium from a population pinned at a floor.

Cumulative Poverty Exposure is aggregate, not longitudinal (new metric, v4.13)

The two Cumulative Poverty Exposure KPIs sum each year's population poverty share, which gives the mean years-poor per agent exactly — but says nothing about how those years are distributed across agents. "Every agent poor for 3 years" and "30% of agents poor for 10 years" produce the same number, and they are very different policy situations. Distinguishing them needs per-agent poverty spells tracked over time, which this engine does not currently keep (agents carry no poverty history, only current state). The related metrics the v4.12 audit also suggested — time-to-tier-attainment per agent, wealth-floor recovery rates, spell-length distributions — all need that same longitudinal layer and remain open; see CONTRIBUTING.md. Note also that the wealth-poverty figure inherits POVERTY_LINE's own undocumented provenance (see Cumulative Bug Fixes and the note at that constant), so it is best read comparatively — Your Settings against Baseline — rather than as an absolute count.

One-at-a-Time (OAT) sensitivity is not Sobol/LHC

OAT sensitivity varies one parameter at a time ±20% of its own slider range (v4.0: uniform across all five parameters — see Cumulative Bug Fixes) and records the poverty and BLEI response from actual model runs. It captures first-order single-parameter effects only. Parameter interactions and higher-order effects are not quantified. For rigorous uncertainty quantification, use the CSV export with external Sobol analysis in Python/R (see CONTRIBUTING.md).

System Stability rewards a stagnant floor as much as a thriving population (updated v4.4)

Historical note: through v4.1 the displayed value was a heuristic, stabBase = improving ? 0.88 : 0.60 plus RNG()×0.08 noise — not derived from wealth variance, BLEI volatility, or agent-state transitions (v3.6 flag). Resolved in v4.2: structuralStability() is the inverse coefficient of variation of median wealth and median BLEI over the run's final quarter — no RNG call, no manual per-mechanism bonus. This formula has its own honest limitation, not previously disclosed here: a population with genuinely flat, unchanging outcomes scores as maximally stable, whether that flatness reflects a thriving equilibrium or a population pinned at a floor with nowhere left to fall — coefficient of variation can't distinguish the two. In the v4.4 N=5,000 study, the Traditional Welfare Baseline — median wealth pinned exactly at WEALTH_FLOOR in 100% of runs by year 20 — scores 99.0% stability, higher than Full Integration's 88.6%, precisely because a hard floor has near-zero variance left to measure. Read this metric as trajectory steadiness, not as an endorsement of the outcome it describes, and always alongside the poverty and BLEI figures next to it, never in isolation. Full N=5,000 comparison in CONTRIBUTING.md.

Main wealth-accumulation loop unit fix — resolved in v4.3, with new open questions

v4.0 fixed wage/USD unit mixing in BLEI, FBS, and Gini but deliberately left the main runYear() wealth-accumulation step on its pre-v4.0 SIU-scale convention, because a full fix proved dangerously sensitive to the conversion constant. v4.3 ships a validated fix (WAGE_TO_USD/LIVING_WAGE_ANNUAL — see Cumulative Bug Fixes, above) after finding an anchor that passes all six VAL_TESTS. This resolves the unit-simplification item but opens two new ones, not yet resolved: whether WEALTH_FLOOR = −$10,000 is still an appropriate floor now that the cost anchor it's measured against roughly doubled (median Baseline wealth now sits exactly at that floor, not just an occasional insolvency case), and whether TARGET_WEALTH/TARGET_POVERTY/TARGET_GINI — set against the old loop — need their own reconsideration against the structurally different new baseline. Both flagged in CONTRIBUTING.md as framework-level decisions, not resolved here — v4.4 adds an in-app pointer to this caveat directly on the affected KPI badges (Gini, Wealth, Stability, Flourishing, EDC — the ⓘ icons), but does not pick new target values. v4.8: a validated N=500-seed sweep across four candidate WEALTH_FLOOR values now exists (see CONTRIBUTING.md) — headline finding: Traditional Welfare Baseline is completely invariant to the floor's exact value on every metric except the raw wealth number itself, while Full Integration shows a real but modest sensitivity (Gini and poverty rate both move a few points as the floor deepens) via agents who dip negative and later recover spending longer at BLEI-liquid-zero the deeper they're allowed to fall first. This is data for a decision, not a decision — the shipped default is unchanged. (Separately: BLEI/FBS/Gini's own wage-to-USD conversion, left on the older SIU_TO_USD approximation since v4.0, is unified onto WAGE_TO_USD in v4.4 — see Cumulative Bug Fixes, above. That's a different unit issue from the one this entry covers.)

Framework does not yet address low-wage populations specifically (new finding, v4.3)

The new Social Security-anchored cohort study (Cumulative Bug Fixes, above) found that Full Integration does not lift a low-wage cohort out of poverty by the simulation's own tier definitions, even though every existing mechanism already applies to it equally — it helps this population enormously in relative terms (originally measured at 20–30× the baseline's BLEI; recomputed at ~7–10× under v4.4's wage-scale unification, since Baseline's own BLEI moved too — see that entry) without coming close in absolute terms. Whether this points to a genuine mechanism gap (e.g. wage-conditional BU scaling) or accurately reflects the framework's intended scope is an open question for the framework's authors, not resolved here — see CONTRIBUTING.md.

CCO conversion proceeds are tracked but not production-constrained

BU → wealth conversion proceeds are credited to agents without debiting a modelled treasury, PTF balance sheet, or production account — external review correctly flagged this as an open stock-flow accounting gap. v4.0 adds transparency only: cumulative conversion proceeds are tracked and exported (CSV/JSON "Conversion Ledger"). A genuine aggregate production constraint — with a defined production function, capacity limits, and rationing behaviour when exhausted — is a further economic-modelling design decision not implemented here; see CONTRIBUTING.md.

No external validation

Parameters calibrated against analogues (Alaska PFD, CLT network, Mondragon) but outcomes not validated against deployment data. Phase 4 roadmap: validation against Fed SCF, CPS, ACS, BLS CES, and OECD inequality trajectories. Required before claims of predictive validity.

Wealth-initialization median cites Fed SCF 2022 but diverges from it by ~5x (new finding, v4.7)

Agent wealth is drawn lognormal(CFG.WEALTH_INIT_MU,CFG.WEALTH_INIT_SIGMA) = lognormal(10.5, 1.2), whose median is exactly e^10.5 ≈ $36K by construction, matching this file's own long-standing "Fed SCF 2022" citation. The actual 2022 Survey of Consumer Finances reports median household net worth at ≈$193K (Kuhn & Ríos-Rull, NBER working paper, 2025, using 2022 SCF microdata) — roughly 5.3x higher, and not close enough to be plausibly explained by survey-vintage drift alone. This is independent of the wealth-init distribution's already-documented inherent Gini ≈0.60 (a function of WEALTH_INIT_SIGMA alone — Gini of a lognormal is 2Φ(σ/√2)−1, with no μ term — so correcting the median would not move that figure); it mainly affects absolute wealth-poverty rates in early simulated years, since POVERTY_LINE is a fixed dollar amount. One caveat worth stating plainly: it's possible $36K was meant to anchor a narrower concept than total net worth (e.g. liquid financial assets) rather than being a plain miscalibration — but no comment anywhere in this file or CONTRIBUTING.md says so, so as written this reads as an uncorrected citation mismatch, not a documented scope choice. Logged as an open calibration: item in CONTRIBUTING.md's Model Architecture Feedback; not changed here — see the note at CFG.WEALTH_INIT_MU/SIGMA, above, for why a fix needs the same mechanics-audit-plus-large-N-restudy treatment as v4.0/v4.3/v4.4 rather than a quiet value swap.
