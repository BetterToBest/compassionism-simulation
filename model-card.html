Model card: the Compassionism Simulation

Describes release v5.9 (engine commit 104691f). Written Nov 17, 2026.

What was frozen and registered, and what was not. The model that was frozen and registered on OSF (osf.io/msdzk) before it ran, on Nov 14, 2026, is the engine 104691f without a central bank. It was first published as release v5.8 (Nov 15, 2026). Release v5.9 (Nov 16) added a central-bank switch (a policy-rate rule, the "Fed" model) and the central-bank study, which has its own preregistration and is not part of the OSF registration. The main results on the pages (500 seeds, central bank off) are the frozen model's. The central-bank and policy-eras results (100 seeds) come from studies run after the freeze.

What it is

A dynamic microsimulation with a reduced-form price level, open and running in the browser. It follows 500 simulated US adults and their children, in households built from the 2022 Survey of Consumer Finances and the Census's Current Population Survey, for 20 or 40 years. Each population is run twice on the same random draws: once with Compassionism, as specified on the Research Hub, and once with no programme. The difference is the programme's effect. It is run in three environments (Reference; Adverse, with recessions, automation and 2% inflation; Stress, with the same and weaker take-up), each 500 times with different draws.

The programme's institutions, firms, lenders and the government are aggregate flows and accounts, not agents. Prices move by one rule for the whole economy, not by markets between agents. Two designs are shown: the full-coverage design (built to meet NEEC's criterion C2.2: unconditional provision covering 100% of basic needs for everyone) and the opt-in design ($1,200 a month, taken up by those for whom it pays).

What it is for, and what not

  • For: showing what the framework's rules imply for poverty, basic living, savings, work, prices and cost, if its stated assumptions hold; making each assumption visible and testable, with every alternative reading behind a switch.
  • Not for: forecasting what would happen in the US, estimating a programme's budget, or reconstructing any past period.

The measures, and how each counts the adults

Of the 500 simulated adults, 18 stand for the very top of the wealth distribution, and their population weights differ widely: 13 carry less than 0.1 each (each stands for far fewer people than one adult), 4 carry more than 2 (the largest is 3.47), and one carries about 1. Together the 18 hold 2.6% of the total weight. The other 482 adults count about once (0.99 to 1.13). Counted this way the 500 adults are as informative as 472.6 adults counted once (the effective sample size), and no adult carries more than 0.69% of the total weight (an adult counted once carries 0.2%). The set of weights is the same in every seed and environment checked; which adults carry them depends on the seed. Weights never enter a decision. Each published measure is one of three kinds:

Counted by weight, the unweighted poverty shares differ by up to about 0.6 points in a single year, less than the spread across runs. The suite page shows plain and weighted averages side by side.

How it was checked

  • The main model was preregistered and frozen before it ran (OSF, osf.io/msdzk), without a central bank; the central-bank and policy-eras studies were preregistered separately before they ran. Every preregistered outcome is published, including the unfavourable ones.
  • Each result across 62 preregistered readings (one assumption changed at a time, and pairs), along the backing curve, and with the programme paid by a contribution instead.
  • Every added part sits behind a switch that, off, reproduces the earlier model exactly; unit tests, accounting identities every year, every part's other reading tested alone and in pairs; a 10,000-adult check.
  • A second implementation of the main row agreed exactly. It was written from the specification and the engine's code and results, so it checks the translation, not a modelling choice the two share.
  • Seed intervals measure the model's randomness only. They do not cover the choice of main row, full backing, or the engine's structure.

Studies beyond the main row (100 paired runs each, not 500)

  • The central bank: a policy-rate rule in both runs, with bond purchases, refinancing, asset prices, job loss and forced sales, in the three environments and in 2005-2025's actual US paths. The rule has no channel to the price of essentials, so the design's claim that it damps the cost of basic living cannot show in this engine. On the findings page.
  • US policy eras: today's households under the measured settings of 1947-1972, 1973-1983, 1984-2007 and 2008-2025. A model counterfactual, not a reconstruction of those years. On the Research Hub.

What this model is not

  • Not a forecast, and not a backtest.
  • No limit on what the economy can supply below full backing; no expectations; one price rule for the whole economy; no migration.
  • A fixed population in the main row; no value of care, community work or new ventures.
  • Community-owned providers (PTFs) are modelled from the Hub's statements and measured costs; the creditor side is not a full accounting of who holds inflation's losses.
  • It measures poverty, cost and work, not the cultural effects Compassionism aspires to.
  • It has not been reviewed by an independent economist. The checklist for a reviewer.
