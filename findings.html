What the simulation finds

The math and code of this simulation were engineered by Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, from the concepts in Duke Johnson's book Better To Best and his related vision for eradicating extreme poverty while enriching cultures and supporting human flourishing. The model has not yet been reviewed by an independent economist; the full code is open for anyone to check, and expert collaborators are welcome.

Three plain results first, then a guided read of each with a chart, then every table behind them with downloads. Pick a release, an environment and 20 or 40 years; the address carries your choice, so any view can be shared.

Layer 1 · The answer

The answer in three numbers #

Below 30 days of basic living

18.7%

with Compassionism, against 46.7% with no programme

Change: −28.0 points

In a typical year, the share of adults whose savings, pay and support would cover fewer than 30 days of basic living (the BLEI paper’s measure).

Below the cost of living

22.0%

with Compassionism, against 55.2% with no programme

Change: −33.2 points

In a typical year, the share of adults whose income falls short of the cost of a basic living (the MIT living-wage basket for one adult).

Too little wealth

35.7%

with Compassionism, against 52.0% with no programme

Change: −16.3 points

At the end of the run, the share of adults with less than $25,000 of savings in today’s money.

Reference environment, 20 years; averages over 500 paired runs of 500 simulated adults (release v5.2 data).
