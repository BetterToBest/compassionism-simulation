# SNAP's work rule for able-bodied adults without dependents (ABAWD time limit): sources

Retrieved Oct 14, 2026, for ledger step s166 (decision d209). Quoted text is the statute as amended; page references are to the sources below.

1. **Food and Nutrition Act of 2008, section 6(o), 7 U.S.C. 2015(o)**, as amended by Public Law 119-21 (July 4, 2025), section 10102. Text read from the
   Legal Information Institute's copy of the U.S. Code (https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/7/2015) and from the enrolled law on govinfo
   (https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ21/html/PLAW-119publ21.htm, 139 Stat. 81-83).
   - (o)(2): no individual is eligible "if, during the preceding 36-month period, the individual received [SNAP] benefits for not less than 3 months
     (consecutive or otherwise) during which the individual did not (A) work 20 hours or more per week, averaged monthly; (B) participate in and comply
     with ... a work program for 20 hours or more per week ...; (C) participate in ... workfare ...; or (D) receive benefits pursuant to paragraph (3), (4),
     (5), or (6)."
   - (o)(3) as amended by P.L. 119-21 sec. 10102(a): the rule does not apply to an individual who is "(A) under 18, or over 65, years of age; (B) medically
     certified as physically or mentally unfit for employment; (C) a parent or other member of a household with responsibility for a dependent child under
     14 years of age; (D) otherwise exempt under subsection (d)(2); (E) a pregnant woman; (F) an Indian or an Urban Indian ...; or (G) a California Indian".
   - (d)(2), the general work-requirement exemptions that (o)(3)(D) carries over: among them (A) a person "currently subject to and complying with a work
     registration requirement under ... the Federal-State unemployment compensation system" (people receiving unemployment insurance); (B) caring for a
     child under six or an incapacitated person; (C) a student at least half time; (D) in drug or alcohol treatment; (E) employed at least 30 hours a week.
   - (o)(4) as amended by sec. 10102(b): area waivers only where unemployment is over 10 percent, or (noncontiguous States) at least 1.5 times the national rate.
   - (o)(5): eligibility is regained by working 80 hours in a 30-day period; after regaining it, a further 3 consecutive months once in any 36 months.
   - (o)(6)(F): a State may exempt up to 8 percent of covered individuals each year (fiscal years 2024 on).
2. **USDA Food and Nutrition Service, "SNAP Provisions of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act of 2025 - ABAWD Exceptions Implementation Memorandum"**,
   Oct 3, 2025 (https://www.fns.usda.gov/snap/obbb-abawd-exemptions-implementation; mirror
   https://www.usda.gov/sites/default/files/guidance-documents/fns.snap-obbb-abawd-exceptions-implementation.pdf). Read through search summaries; the
   pages refused a direct download from this environment (HTTP 403). It states: the time limit now applies to ages 18 to 64 (USDA's reading of "over 65");
   the dependent-child exception now covers children under 14 only; the exceptions the Fiscal Responsibility Act of 2023 added (homeless individuals,
   veterans, young adults leaving foster care) are removed; effective Nov 1, 2025.
3. **USDA FNS, "OBBB ABAWD Waivers Implementation Memorandum"**, Oct 3, 2025 (https://www.fns.usda.gov/snap/obbb-abawd-waivers-implementation): waivers
   need an unemployment rate over 10 percent (12-month or 3-month average); FNS expects very few areas to qualify.
