# The outside audit before the Runpod study: what was fixed, what waits, what was turned down

Oct 13, 2026 (ledger step s154). Two outside AI reviewers (GPT and Grok) read the health-financing reports (v5-27 and v5-28), the decision log, the handoff note and the engine before the paid 500-seed study on a rented server (Runpod; ledger step s137). This note sorts every finding into three groups: **fix before the study** (done in this session), **fix later** (recorded, with the reason it can wait) and **turned down** (with the reason). No engine code changed; the changes are to the study's design, the tools that run it, and the wording of two reports. Choices are recorded in `dev/DECISIONS.md`, Session 2d.

Labels as in the other reports: **sourced** (from a published source), **simulated** (an output of the model's runs), **derived** (arithmetic on sourced or simulated figures), **design value** (a modelling choice, swept).

## What the reviewers got right that matters most

- **The cost-of-living line is derived, not a published MIT number.** The $49,370 a year for one adult is the plain (unweighted) average of the 51 state figures in MIT's Living Wage Calculator (its February 2026 data, one adult, no children; checked by hand for five states in an earlier session). MIT publishes no single national figure. Weighting the states by population would give about $51,210, 3.7% higher (an outside recomputation, partly checked; not adopted). The reports called the figure "sourced"; they now say "derived" and give the rule.
- **Setting C's arithmetic sentence was wrong.** Setting C is single payer with a private layer, paid like Medicare Advantage. The report said "20% is the high case of both together (1.08 x 1.11)". But 1.08 x 1.11 is the coding high case with selection at its central value (19.9%). Both high together is 1.08 x 1.165, which is 25.8%, and that is what the table's "both high" row ran. Also, the central case (1.04 x 1.11 = 15.4%) does not reproduce MedPAC's 14% total. MedPAC's total also carries a part of Medicare Advantage's payment rules (bids and bonuses) that, by these figures, comes to about 1% below traditional Medicare (derived: 1.14 / 1.154 = 0.988). The model has no counterpart to that part. So the central case sits 1.4 points above MedPAC's total by construction; it is not a match. The wording is corrected. Duke accepted the earlier wording (ledger decision d193); this is an arithmetic correction to it, flagged for his reading.

## Fix later (recorded)

- **A. A run at the population-weighted cost-of-living line (GPT 1).** The line drives the yearly wealth update, every household's basket, and SNAP's shelter and medical lines, so changing it is a change to the released model's anchor. It needs its own audited step: re-derive the weighted figure from MIT's data directly, check that every dollar path scales with it, then run both. Both runs in a comparison share the line, so Compassionism's effect moves less than the poverty levels do.
- **B. PTF's price cut on medical care and single payer's price savings (GPT 4).** In every main row PTF's health-sector cut is 0, so nothing can overlap there. In the 25% reading under single payer the cut is applied on top of the Yale preprint's savings, and those already include lower prices. That reading is now labelled an upper reading. A full accounting of each saving, with a test, comes before that reading is ever used for a headline.
- **C. SNAP's work rules (GPT 7).** The model leaves out SNAP's time limit for adults without dependants, and the 2025 law (Public Law 119-21) widened it (adults to 64, parents whose youngest child is 14 or older). Leaving it out makes today's safety net more generous than it is in both runs. The limits section now says so; modelling the rule is a later engine step.
- **D. Separate take-up for the Child Tax Credit (GPT 8).** Today both credits share one take-up draw. A separate draw is a later engine step; the study's take-up rows bound it for now.
- **E. Report framing for the results (GPT 5, GPT 6, Grok 6, 7, 8).** In the study's results report: present single payer's results as conditional on the Yale preprint, with the range beside them; keep poverty results and government's costs as two separate tables, with the safety net's outlays labelled as not financed in the model; label the Yale preprint's $100 billion of avoided emergency and inpatient care as an input from a preprint; repeat the note that "too little wealth" rises under Compassionism in every cell for the reason the v5.4 release gave (people hold less cash once the basket is covered), not because of health financing; and caption each table that the safety net's small rise under setting A comes through the model's prices.
- **F. An outside calibration of the price response (GPT 9).** The study's flat and 1.5-times rows give the range; finding outside evidence for how strongly prices respond to benefit-funded demand is later work.

## Turned down (with reasons)

- **"The high case is 25.8%, so the report understated it" (GPT 2), in part.** The table already ran both high (25.8%, "both high", 50% of B's savings eroded in Reference). What was wrong was the sentence naming 1.08 x 1.11 as both high; that is corrected. The coding range (0-8%) stays a design range. The reviewer says MedPAC's 2025 8% referred to bids and 10% to payments; that could not be checked here, so the 8% is labelled a design value near MedPAC's 2025 estimate.
- **"Benefits may be double counted against the cost of living's tax line" (Grok 2).** Today's safety net is in both runs of every comparison, so it cannot add to Compassionism's effect. The tax line is the same average for everyone in both runs (a known limit, already reported). Benefits are counted as income once. What is missing is a revenue model (fix-before item 5 and later item E), not a double count.
- **"Use FY2027 SNAP amounts" (GPT 7).** The model's dollars are 2025 dollars, and FY2026 amounts match them. Using FY2027 amounts would mix price years. Turned down, with the price year now stated.
- **"Different branches' random draws may break the pairing" (GPT 10).** Each person's eight draws a year come from the same stream in both runs (the guarantee every release checks), and the health and safety-net draws come from a hash of seed, person, year and slot, the same in both runs. Turned down as already in place.
- **"Morris can miss interactions; go straight to Sobol" (Grok 5).** Morris's spread measure (sigma) flags interactions, and Sobol follows on the leaders. Kept as designed, with the noise check added.
- **A separate engine option charging the overpayment only for people 65 and over (Grok 1).** The study's no-overpayment row bounds it from below and the central row from above, so the option would add engine code without widening the range shown.

## What the study runs (ledger step s137)

All from one pinned commit, seeds 1-500, paired, in the Reference, Adverse and Stress environments (`dev/tools/s137_jobs.sh`):

- **The health financing table.** The six cells (settings A, B and C, each without and with Compassionism, today's safety net in each) and the no-programme, no-safety-net line, plus: the safety-net row without a health setting; government's share floating; the prevention link's central case; single payer's cautious and high cost cases (B and C); setting C at 25% and 75% enrolment and at no, 19.9% and 25.8% overpayment; today's safety net at low and high take-up.
- **The price channel.** The six cells with prices flat and with every price response 1.5 times.
- **Sensitivity.** Morris (20 paths, two seed blocks of 10) in each environment, joined with the noise check; Sobol in Stress on v5.4's six leaders.

About 60,000 core-seconds (17 core-hours at 0.43 s per run for health rows and 0.35 s otherwise): about 1.2 hours on a 16-core server, under $1 at the server's hourly price. The spend cap stays $8.

The study's comparison restudy (named in the plan) is not in this job: no file in the repository defines it beyond the name, so it is left for the session that writes the study's results, rather than guessed.
