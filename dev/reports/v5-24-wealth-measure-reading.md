# How to read "too little wealth" (v5.4.1)

Oct 12, 2026 · Claude · v5.4.1 · words only; no figure changes

## What you asked

You asked for a note beside the wealth result explaining a change of mindset. Today people build savings as a reserve against a lost job or an emergency. Under Compassionism next month's basics are guaranteed, and work becomes more of a choice. Many people would then save less and spend more of their time and money on a life of passion, hobbies and travel. The gain would show up in surveys of how people live, not in dollars.

## What the page now says

A short note, "How to read this measure", sits directly under the Savings result on the release page. It says four things:

1. **What the measure counts.** Adults whose net wealth is below six months of their own cost of living, which is a reserve against setbacks. Today that reserve is sensible.
2. **Your framing.** With the basics guaranteed, people need the reserve less, so smaller savings would not mean less security, and the gain would be measured in surveys more than in dollars. It is attributed to you and to the Research Hub's glossary entry "Post-Scarcity Economics".
3. **Outside evidence for part of it.** Economists have found that a guaranteed minimum lowers how much lower-income households save for emergencies (Hubbard, Skinner and Zeldes, *Journal of Political Economy*, 1995).
4. **What the model does not do.** In the model, people spend the same share of their income above their costs with or without the programme (the share that matches today's US saving rate). So nobody in the model saves less because they feel secure. The model also measures no well-being by survey. The model's wealth gap comes from its mechanics: prices rise while savings earn nothing, and people take lower pay after a job loss. The note says the framing explains how to read the measure, and it does not turn the result into a gain.

## Why point 4 is there

Without it, a reader could think the model shows people choosing to save less. It does not. If it did, the wealth figure would be *worse*, not better, because the measure counts savings. Saying so keeps the note honest. It also protects your framing from the criticism that it explains away a bad number. The verdict line under the results ("Not a confirmed gain") is unchanged.

## What could come later

- **Survey-style measures of well-being.** Time freed from work, hours given to community projects and participation are already on the plan ("Simple measures of what money does not capture"). They would let the model show the side of the result your framing points to.
- **A lower-saving reading.** A reading where people save less once the basics are guaranteed could be added as a swept assumption once a measured rate is found. It would show how much of the wealth gap is a choice rather than a loss.
