# How Compassionism pays for itself: Claude's reading of the design

Oct 1, 2026 · plan step 3, written before any code · Claude

Duke, this is my reading of what the Research Hub says about where the money comes from. Please say if it misrepresents your design. Where the Hub is silent I chose the most cautious option and say so.

## What the Hub says

1. **Basic Units are created, not collected.** The model legislation calls them "the supplementary currency created under this Act, pegged 1:1 to US dollars", and they are paid "automatically through Treasury Direct Deposit" (Integrated Implementation Roadmap, Appendix A, Sections 101-102). The parliamentary template says "the Minister may authorize issuance of basic units" (Integrated Digital Governance, Appendix B.1).
2. **New money enters through conversion.** The dual-currency paper writes the money supply as last year's money plus everything converted this year, and says conversion is limited by octave capacity, "creating an endogenous ceiling on monetary expansion" (Dual Currency Systems and Inflation, 3.4). The corporate paper calls this a "full creative reserve": money created through human contribution instead of through debt (Corporate Transformation, 1.3).
3. **The conversion tax is the system's revenue.** The BLEI paper says that below about 60% participation, "conversion tax revenue may be insufficient to fund the $1,200/adult BU allocation at national scale without external fiscal support", and estimates self-funding at 60-65% participation with a 12% conversion tax. It also says a formal fiscal model is still needed (BLEI paper, 6.3 and 9).
4. **Unspent BU expire.** They go to projects and are converted there at the holder's earned rate (Hub rule R2, already in the model). The dual-currency paper says that in equilibrium the BU distributed equal the BU that expire.
5. **No wage tax is described for the BU.** The roadmap mentions "progressive taxation and efficiency gains" only as a precondition for the whole programme. The flat contribution on wages that the model uses today is the model's own stand-in.

## My reading, as rules the model can run

- **S1. A Source (the Treasury) issues the BU.** Issuing a BU costs nothing in dollars until it is converted.
- **S2. Every conversion is paid by the Source.** When an essential-service business, a project hirer or an ESP worker converts BU, the Source pays BU × rate in dollars and keeps the conversion tax. This includes the face value of the BU businesses accept for essentials: the business receives dollars from the Source for them.
- **S3. The Source's net dollar payout is new money.** The Hub names no other source, so this is the cautious reading (labelled a modelling assumption). New money raises prices through the model's price rule, except the part backed by new output. The new output is what plan step 2 built: the capital the ESP split reinvests, and project work counted at the face value of the hours it pays for.
- **S4. The conversion tax returns to the Source.** It is withheld from each payout, so it lowers the new money directly.
- **S5. Expired BU return to the Source** and are directed to projects (as now).
- **S6. The flat wage contribution is removed** for everything the Source pays. What the Source does not pay (the PTF and PTH price cuts, and PTH appreciation) stays financed by the contribution until plan step 5 costs those programmes properly.

## What this means before any result

- Under S3 the inflation the model shows comes from how much is converted, at what rates, and how much new output backs it. The earlier runs with created conversion money ("hybrid" financing) showed very high inflation (about 23-30 points a year). That is likely to come back here, and the checkpoint report will show it plainly.
- The Hub's self-funding claim cannot be checked from the text: it needs the fiscal model the BLEI paper says is missing. The model will report the Source's ledger each year: BU issued, dollars paid, tax kept, new money, the part backed by output, and how much of the BU issued the tax would cover.

## Sensitivity rows kept

- The flat contribution (today's financing).
- H1: every dollar the Source pays backed by output (no inflation from the programme).
- The face value of essentials bought with BU counted as backed by output (the essentials were produced and sold), so only the conversion premium is new money.
