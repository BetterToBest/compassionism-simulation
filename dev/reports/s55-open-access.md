## The words used here

- **Open access** (Duke, Nov 7): anyone may shop at a Public Trust Foundation (PTF) business, in dollars or Basic Units, at one posted price. In the model, every adult in a zone buys the zone's **PTF share of essential sales** at PTF prices, and the Hub's two density tests read that share: the food price cut is smaller below 40%, and the synergy coefficient θ is zero below 55%. Without the community layer the whole population is one zone. **Members only** is the frozen batch's model: only PTF members pay PTF prices, and the tests count members.
- **The growth rule** (main values): the share starts at the environment's starting member share and grows each year as far as three limits allow: **capacity** (the capital the PTFs own: the starting share plus capital per adult ÷ $18,862, in year-0 dollars), **operations** (sales must cover running costs; in the Hub's own cost structure PTFs price at cost, so this limit cannot bind), and **demand** (a ceiling of 49%, S Group's share of Finland's grocery sales in 2025; a labelled design parameter, swept at 25%, 75% and 100%). The share never falls.
- **The C2.2 design** (headline, Duke's answer to ledger decision D8) and **the release design** ($1,200 a month, opt-in) are both shown. The five parts: the CCO (the Basic Unit), PTF, PTH (housing), SZH (zones), CIP (community investment).
- **Outcomes**, as in the study programme: *basic living* (share of adult-years under 30 days of basic living, design-neutral reading; the design's own reading is in the tables and never claimed), *cost of living* (share of adult-years under the MIT living-wage budget), *wealth* (share of adults with wealth under $25,000 in year-0 prices at year 20 or 40), *cost* (programme cost per adult-year, year-0 dollars), *inflation* (the yearly price rise implied by the price level). For the three shares a reduction is a positive number of percentage points ("points"), so a positive "open − members" figure means open access helps.
- **The rule** (addendum rule 1): an open-access effect is stated only where its 95% interval excludes zero, and called **material** only at 1 point or more (5% of the members-only row's cost for cost; 1 point of the yearly inflation rate for inflation). Intervals are 95% paired-bootstrap intervals over seeds (10,000 replicates, bootstrap seed 20261101). Because the comparison is paired on 500 seeds, effects of a few hundredths of a point are detectable; "stated" alone does not mean "large".

## The short answers

1. **Open access barely moves basic living and the cost of living; it moves wealth materially under the C2.2 design.** In the headline cell (C2.2 design, Reference, 10,000 adults, 20 years, CPS yardstick, octave raise off), letting every adult buy at PTF prices improves basic living by 0.03 points [0.03, 0.03] and the cost of living by 0.14 points [0.14, 0.14], adds $1,398 per adult-year to the cost [1,396, 1,400] (2.3% of the members-only row's cost) and lowers inflation by 0.64 points a year [0.64, 0.64]. None of these is material. On wealth it is material: 2.42 points [2.40, 2.44] fewer adults with wealth under $25,000 at year 20. With members only the programme left slightly more adults with low wealth than no programme (−0.19 points [−0.23, −0.14]); with open access it leaves fewer (+2.23 [2.19, 2.28]). That is the one programme decision that changes.
2. **This holds across the C2.2 settings, not under the release design.** Over all 24 settings (both designs, three environments, 500 and 10,000 adults, 20 and 40 years) every one of the 120 open-access effects on the claimable outcomes has an interval excluding zero, and 14 are material: wealth under the C2.2 design in every setting but Stress at 40 years (+1.28 to +3.12 points), and inflation under the C2.2 design in Adverse and Stress at 40 years (−1.24 to −1.64 points a year). On basic living and the cost of living the largest open-access effects are +0.27 and +0.52 points (C2.2, Stress, 40 years). Under the release design none is material: basic living +0.02 to +0.23, the cost of living −0.03 to +0.46, wealth −0.77 to +0.36, cost +$151 to +$861, inflation −0.14 to +0.27 points a year. 500 and 10,000 adults lead to the same decision in all 60 comparisons.
3. **The wealth result depends on the growth rule's settings, so the headline sentence names them (rule 2).** With the demand ceiling at 25%, or with outside running costs, or with growth off, open access's wealth effect disappears under the C2.2 design (−0.62 to +0.48 points; never material). With the ceiling at 75% or 100% it grows (Reference, 500 adults, 20 years: +5.15 and +7.63 points), the cost becomes material (+$3,025 and +$4,272, 5.0% and 7.1%) and so does the fall in inflation (−1.33 and −1.91 points a year). Under the release design a high ceiling turns the wealth effect materially unfavourable in Adverse and Stress (−1.09 to −1.98 points). The headline sentence is therefore: *"With the PTFs' share of essential sales growing to a 49% demand ceiling, open access leaves 2.4 points fewer adults with low wealth after 20 years under the C2.2 design (Reference, 10,000 adults); its size depends on the demand ceiling, a design parameter, and on the share being able to grow. Open access changes basic living and the cost of living by less than 0.3 and 0.6 points in every setting."*
4. **Which limit binds: capacity first, then demand; operations never in the main reading.** Under the C2.2 design in Reference and Adverse, capacity holds the share near 46-48% at year 5 and every seed reaches the 49% ceiling by year 10. In Stress the share is about 20% at year 5 and reaches the ceiling by year 10 on 86-100% of seeds and on all by year 19. Under the release design the share is about 38-39% at year 5 in Reference and Adverse and at the ceiling by year 10; in Stress capacity stops growth in 98-100% of the first 20 years' adult-years (the share is 12% at year 5, 19% at year 10, 42% at year 19) and the ceiling is reached only by year 39. With outside running costs no zone ever grows (the PTFs' sales at their prices cover 35-59% of running costs at outside margins), and the results equal those with growth off. At the 49% ceiling the 55% test is never passed.
5. **The fixed-share curve rises smoothly with no step at the 55% test, and one small step at the 40% test.** Holding every zone's share at 10%, ..., 100% (headline cell): wealth goes from +1.16 to +7.87 points against no programme, inflation from 34.72 to 32.49 points a year, cost from $60,617 to $66,768; basic living moves 17.62 to 17.70 points and the cost of living 24.42 to 24.95. A step at a test is claimed in 2 of 180 cases, both on wealth at the 40% test under the C2.2 design in Adverse at 20 years (+1.18 [1.10, 1.26] at 500 adults, +1.20 [1.18, 1.22] at 10,000). There is no step at the 55% test anywhere. 149 further steps are detectable but below the threshold.
6. **Synergy: open access's wealth gain is a CCO × PTF interaction.** On basic living and the cost of living S36's answer stands: the CCO is almost the whole effect, and the parts overlap (mostly CCO × CIP). On wealth, under the C2.2 design, CCO × PTF becomes a material synergy in 6 of 9 settings (Reference and Adverse, both horizons and sizes; members only: none), +1.43 points [1.42, 1.45] in the headline cell (members only −0.99). The whole-design contrast on wealth is a material synergy in 8 of 9 C2.2 settings (members only: 2 synergies, 2 overlaps). PTF × CIP's wealth synergy, material in 4 settings with members only, falls below materiality in all. On inflation the whole design inflates materially less than its parts in all 9 C2.2 settings (members only: 5). Under the release design little changes. In all, 137 of 990 contrast decisions change, 34 across the materiality line.
7. **The community layer still changes nothing material.** Under open access the layer's own effect (reading the tests in each community, with zones placed as SZH places them) is at most 0.02 points on basic living, 0.03 on the cost of living, 0.17 on wealth, $109 on cost and 0.04 points a year on inflation: 0 of 120 material, as with members only. One swept layer setting has a material effect: zones made entirely of members (f = 1), C2.2, Adverse, 500 adults, 20 years, wealth +1.39 points [1.31, 1.46] (in Reference +0.89, below materiality). The PTF's starting size (10%, 30%, 50%) makes no material difference to the layer's effect.

- **Correction note (Nov 10, 2026, S66; no figure changed).** The damping half of the reading above is wrong for these rows. The release rows are built with PTF and PTH damping of outside inflation switched off (`n1Row` sets `noDamp` unless a row asks for damping; decision d86), so the damping acts in no row of this report, in any environment. S66 switched the damping line off on its own and every output was identical in Reference and Adverse (`dev/reports/s66-low-share-trace.md`). What the share does move under the C2.2 design is the PTF price cut every adult gets (a larger share: lower inflation, more wealth) and the ESP surplus split (the PTFs' part of the business premium, and the year their capital account reaches its full-capacity mark; S66 section 1). Those two, not damping, are the traced channels.

## 6. The community layer under open access (Q26)

- **The layer's effect on the release row (Q7).** Open release row with the layer minus without it: at most 0.02 points on basic living, 0.03 on the cost of living, 0.17 on wealth, $109 on cost and 0.04 points a year on inflation, in any setting; 0 of 120 material (members only: 0 as well, S38). It differs from its members-only value by at most 0.11 points. At 2,000 adults the layer's effect under open access cannot be formed: the addendum ran the open row with the layer at 2,000 adults (study VJ) but no open row without it. That row is compared with the members-only row with the layer (study J) instead; nothing was run outside the preregistration to fill the gap. Open access with the layer, against members only with the layer, is material in 21 of 180 outcome-settings, the same wealth and inflation pattern as Q24.
- **The synergy table with the layer (Q8)** under open access follows Q25: CCO × PTF on wealth material in 6 of 9 C2.2 settings (members only with the layer: none), the whole design in 8 of 9. 136 of 990 contrast decisions change; 30 cross the materiality line.
- **The layer's design parameters (Q9).** One row has a material layer effect under open access: zones made entirely of members (f = 1), C2.2, Adverse, 500 adults, 20 years, wealth +1.39 [1.31, 1.46]. Those zones start at a share of 100%, so they pass both tests from year 1. In Reference the same row gives +0.89, below materiality. The programme's own decision changes in 2 outcome-settings: zones that need CIP, without the CCO, Stress, 20 years, wealth (not stated → a small gain), both designs.
- **The PTF's starting size (Q10).** At starting shares of 10%, 30% and 50%, the layer's effect under open access differs by less than 1 point between any two shares on every claimable outcome (0 material).
- **Why the layer still does little (Claude's reading):** without the layer the whole population's share grows past the 40% test within a few years and stops at 49%, below the 55% test. The layer only spreads that share unevenly among zones, which moves few adults across either test.

## 7. What this does and does not show

- **The limits stated with these results** (addendum, Reporting): the demand ceiling (49%) is a labelled design parameter, not a sourced figure for PTFs; capacity is counted for the population, not zone by zone; the share never falls (no rule closes a PTF); shopping in another zone is not modelled; the conversion-rate bonus stays with members, and the model has no PTF workforce; the price cuts and the two density tests are the Hub's figures, used as given.
- **Growth in the model is fast.** The C2.2 design's Source flows build the PTFs' capital quickly, so capacity reaches 100% by year 10 in Reference and Adverse. A real network would face limits the model does not have (sites, staff, suppliers, permits). The fixed-share curve (section 4) is the way to read the results without relying on that speed.
- **Every effect is through prices.** Open access gives every adult the PTF price cut on the share of purchases the PTFs supply, and lets the tests and the inflation damping read that share. It does not add jobs, wages or community effects; the model has none for PTF.
- **40-year statements hold without ageing only**; no ageing rows were run under open access.
- **Nulls and unfavourable results are reported in full:** basic living and the cost of living barely move in any setting; under the release design wealth gets slightly worse in 8 of 12 settings (−0.21 to −0.77 points) and materially worse with a high demand ceiling in Adverse and Stress; the cost rises in every setting.
- **What it means for the release (S7) and the Hub.** The release carries both readings behind the switch (S55's plan). The Hub's PTF figures (S25, S40) should rest on open access, the reading Duke has confirmed. Wherever they cite the wealth result they should carry rule 2's sentence: it depends on the demand ceiling and on the share being able to grow. S36 and S38 stand as published for members only; their basic-living and cost-of-living answers also hold under open access.
- Every cell is published: all settings of T, U, U10, V, V10, VJ, W, W10 and Y are in `tables.md`, and every estimate in `estimates.csv`.
