A screen ranks readings. It does not estimate the programme's effect: its points are off the main row and its seeds are few. No figure in this note is a headline figure.

## In short

1. **What it is for.** The v5.9 model added many readings, each tested on its own in its own step. The 500-seed batch cannot carry every combination, and a reading that matters only together with another is missed by tests one at a time. This screen moves all of them together (the Morris method, 12 random paths through the box of readings) and asks which ones move the programme's effect on each headline outcome by a material amount. The v5.9 preregistration then carries those readings, and drops the rest only with this evidence.
2. **At full backing, the main row's reading of the design, the box is calm.** Across every point of the box (408 points), the programme lowers basic-living poverty, cost-of-living poverty and wealth poverty in both designs and both environments. No point reverses any of the three. Programme inflation stays between 0 and 0.2 points a year. The readings that move a poverty outcome by a point or more are few: the income effect (earnings forgone per unconditional dollar) for the opt-in design, and how adults outside the labour force convert and move in and out of work for the full-coverage design's cost-of-living poverty. Median real wealth moves much more, mainly through the share of spare cash households spend (the programme's effect on the median ranges from $65,000 to $717,000 across the box).
3. **With no assumed backing, the model runs away, and almost everything matters.** Where only the output the model sees sold matches the payout, programme inflation runs at 30 to 142 points a year across the box. The opt-in design's effect on poverty reverses sign at one point in ten to one in five (cost-of-living poverty at 36 of 408 points in Reference, 43 in Adverse; wealth poverty at 61 and 87), and the full-coverage design's wealth-poverty effect at 16 and 32. The largest movers are wage indexation (how far wages follow the programme's prices), the price rule's response to excess demand, and how far real assets follow inflation. In a runaway, small changes in how fast prices and wages chase each other change everything downstream.
4. **The financed rows (the programme paid for by a contribution) also run away in much of the box.** Where the contribution raises less than the full cost and the rest is borrowed or printed, programme debt reaches up to 1.6 million percent of GDP by year 20 and inflation up to 135 points a year. The share the contribution is asked to raise and how the rest is met dominate every outcome. These numbers show that the readings move a runaway; they are not estimates of anything.
5. **The rules, applied as written, carry most readings.** Of the 33 readings screened on the Source rows, 22 must be carried at both ends of their range, 3 at one reading, and 8 may be held at the main reading. Of the 41 screened on the financed rows, 34 are carried in full (one of them by the agreement rule), 1 at one reading, and 6 held. Most of the readings carried owe it to the no-backing and financed cells; on the full-backing cells alone, 9 would reach the threshold. The rules say "any cell", so the long list stands.
6. **Readings that never matter here.** The creditor side's three readings (who bears the inflated debt, the share left outside the model, the model's families as the whole household sector), the PTF reserve, the width of the density tests' band, the borrowing limit for ordinary spending, the tail exponent and the recession slack stay below half the threshold in every cell of both blocks. This agrees with each one's own step. The borrowing limit is held on the Source rows but carried on the financed rows, where it moves the debt.
7. **Interactions are the norm, not the exception.** Most carried readings have effects that depend on where the others sit (28 pairs on the Source rows, 66 on the financed rows meet the preregistered flag). The pairs that recur most: how far real assets follow inflation with the interest pass-through, with wage indexation and with the repricing of fixed-rate debt; the price rule's demand response with wage indexation; on the financed rows, the share financed with how the rest is met. These are flags for the v5.9 preregistration's design, not claims of synergy.

## What the screen did

- **Readings.** Every swept reading of the v5.9 model in the list the assumptions screen's plan fixed: the price rule (demand response κ, persistence ρ, weight on wage indexation α, normal-years slack, recession slack, velocity drift), the share of spare cash spent, wage indexation, the income effect, the PTF demand ceiling, the borrowing limit, the participation readings (conversion outside the labour force, yearly flows, the share of the income effect taken by leaving work), wealth by asset class (interest pass-through, where new saving goes, how far real assets follow inflation, repricing of fixed-rate debt), bargaining (pass-through and markdown), the PTF's operations (running cost, reserve, band width, what the tests read, hiring limits, who gets the bonus, who pays), the creditor side (three readings), and the weighted tail (cutoff, exponent, households per type). Block F adds the financing readings (share financed, base, cap, how the rest is met, the interest rate readings, the policy-rate rule and its three coefficients, who pays the interest) and drops the tail, which those rows run without.
- **Rows.** Block S: the no-programme run; both designs at full backing (the main row, Duke's decision of Nov 14) and at no assumed backing (the other end of the backing curve). Block F: both designs paid for by a contribution.
- **Effect.** The programme's run minus its own no-programme run at the same point, mean over seeds (median for the two debt outcomes). An elementary effect is the change in that effect when one reading moves two thirds of its range (or switches category). μ* (the mean size of these changes over 12 paths) ranks the readings; μ gives the direction; σ, cleaned of seed noise, flags interaction.
- **Materiality** (fixed before the run): 1 point for the three poverty outcomes, 1 point a year of programme inflation, 5% of the no-programme median real wealth (about $16,700 in Reference and $12,000 in Adverse on the Source rows), 10% of GDP of debt at year 20, and 2 years in the year debt passes GDP.

## What moves each headline

On the full-backing cells (the main row's reading; the v5.9 preregistration's main specification curve is drawn here):

| outcome | Full coverage | opt-in design |
|---|---|---|
| basic-living poverty | nothing reaches 1 point (largest: the spending share 0.6, the weighted tail's cutoff 0.6 in Reference; how far real assets follow inflation 0.8 in Adverse) | the income effect 0.9 (Reference), 1.05 (Adverse) |
| cost-of-living poverty | conversion outside the labour force 2.2; the yearly labour-force flows 1.4 (Reference); the income effect 0.9-1.0 | the income effect 2.1-2.4; leaving work's share of the income effect 0.9 (Reference) |
| wealth poverty (yearly average) | nothing reaches 1 point (the income effect 0.7-0.8, the tail's cutoff 0.7-0.8) | the income effect 1.2-1.25 |
| median real wealth | the spending share ($267,000-$295,000), where new saving goes ($51,000-$54,000), the income effect ($38,000-$42,000) | the spending share ($73,000-$115,000), the income effect, where new saving goes |
| programme inflation | nothing above 0.04 points a year | nothing above 0.02 |

(Figures are μ*, in the outcome's units.)

On the no-assumed-backing cells, wage indexation leads programme inflation (μ* 20 to 34 points a year), followed by the price rule's demand response (8 to 14), the yearly labour-force flows, the velocity drift and the income effect. For wealth poverty and median wealth the leader is how far real assets follow inflation (8 to 13 points of wealth poverty; $150,000 to $226,000 of median wealth), then the interest pass-through and the repricing of fixed-rate debt: in a runaway, who holds what kind of asset decides who keeps their wealth.

On the financed rows, the share the contribution is asked to raise leads or comes second in almost every cell, with how the rest is met; wage indexation and how far real assets follow inflation come next for the household outcomes; the interest-rate readings and who pays the interest lead the debt.

## The decision rules, as preregistered

Rule 2 carries a reading at both ends of its range (each category) if its μ* reaches the threshold on any primary outcome in any cell; rule 3 carries one reading if it reaches half the threshold somewhere; rule 4 holds it at the main reading otherwise. Rule 6 reads the price rule's six readings only on the no-assumed-backing cells (at full backing almost no new money reaches prices), the markdown only on steps where the bargaining pass-through is above 0, and the policy-rate rule's coefficients only under that rule.

**Source rows (block S, 33 readings).**
- Carry in full (22): wage indexation, the spending share, how far real assets follow inflation, the price rule's demand response, the yearly labour-force flows, the velocity drift, the interest pass-through, where new saving goes, the income effect, the price rule's persistence, its weight on wage indexation, the repricing of fixed-rate debt, who pays the PTF cut's running cost, the markdown, the bargaining pass-through, conversion outside the labour force, the PTF running cost, the PTF demand ceiling, leaving work's share of the income effect, the PTF hiring limit, what the density tests read, and who gets the conversion bonus.
- Carry at one reading (3): the weighted tail's cutoff at the 90th percentile; six tail households per type; the normal-years slack at 0.0446. Each is the end with the larger mean effect in the cell where the reading came nearest the threshold (the full-coverage design's cost-of-living poverty, Adverse); for the tail households and the slack the main reading sits at one end, so there is only one other.
- Hold at main (8), with the largest μ* as a share of the threshold: who bears the inflated debt (0.28), the share left outside (0.28), the band width (0.13), the borrowing limit (0.10), the tail exponent (0.06), the families as the whole household sector (0.03), the reserve (0.01), and the recession slack (0.001; it barely acts without the spending layer, as the preregistration expected: its largest μ* is $21 of median wealth).

**Financed rows (block F, 41 readings).**
- Carry in full (34): every reading carried in full on the Source rows except the markdown, plus the borrowing limit, the normal-years slack, the share financed, the base, the cap, how the rest is met, the fixed real rate, the debt premium, the rate reading (fixed or the policy-rate rule), the rule's response to inflation and its neutral rate, who pays the interest, and (by rule 7, below) the rule's response to the output gap.
- Carry at one reading (1): the markdown at 1 (no markdown).
- Hold at main (6): the band width (0.37), the share left outside (0.28), who bears the inflated debt (0.26), the families as the whole household sector (0.15), the recession slack (0.10), and the reserve (0.02).

**The noise check.** Every reading carried on the Source rows reaches its largest μ* in a cell where it is measured (the two seed blocks agree within a quarter). On the financed rows, six carried readings peak on the debt outcome in a cell where the seed blocks disagree (the yearly labour-force flows, the borrowing limit, leaving work's share, the price rule's weight on wage indexation, the bargaining pass-through, what the tests read); rule 2 carries them all the same.

**Interactions (rule 5).** A carried reading flagged for interaction in a cell where it reaches the threshold is to be carried crossed with the top-ranked reading of that cell (a 2 × 2 beside the curve), or the v5.9 preregistration states why not. That gives 28 pairs on the Source rows and 66 on the financed rows (both tables list them with the number of cells). On the full-backing cells alone, four pairs, all on median wealth with the spending share: the interest pass-through, where new saving goes, how far real assets follow inflation, and the markdown.

**Agreement with the one-at-a-time steps (rule 7).**
- The steps' large movers are carried here: leaving work's share and the yearly flows (over 1 point in its pilot), the price rule's persistence and velocity drift, and the rate rule against the fixed rate (13-15% of the full-coverage design's debt).
- The held readings agree with their steps: the creditor side left wealth poverty unchanged in the creditor study (within 0.1 point); the reserve and the band width moved little in the PTF-realism study; the borrowing limit changed how deep households fall into debt, not a headline, in the baseline-scope study; the tail exponent moved the top 1% share in the top-tail study, which this screen does not rank. One difference of size, not of verdict: the creditor study's pilot, where inflation ran 30-45% a year, found that leaving nothing outside the model lowered the median adult's wealth gain from $31,500 to $21,200 (about 0.6 of this screen's threshold); here the same reading stays below 0.3.
- **One reading is carried by this rule.** The policy-rate rule's response to the output gap stays below a twentieth of the threshold here, but the government-budget study moved the rule's two weights together and reported the debt moving by 5-16%, far above 10% of GDP. The screen cannot tell from the government-budget study's figures how much of that was this weight, so it is carried on the financed rows (the cautious reading).
- The reverse case: the bargaining pass-through (the bargaining study: 0.1-0.6 points of poverty at its bound) and the PTF's operating readings (the PTF-realism study: 0.1-1.0 points) reach the threshold here only in the no-backing and financed cells, through inflation and debt; at full backing the pass-through reaches 0.98 of the threshold on median wealth and the PTF readings stay below, apart from who gets the bonus (1.09, median wealth). They are carried; nothing is added.

## What the v5.9 preregistration must carry (written into research-update PLAN the v5.9 preregistration)

- **The main specification curve (full backing) and the backing curve:** the 22 Source-row readings at each end or category, the three one-reading points, and the 2 × 2 pairs (or why not). That is 32 single-reading points beside the main row on each curve, plus the pairs and the least-favourable bound. On the full-backing curve the price rule's six readings are expected to sit within seed noise of the main row (their μ* there is below 0.05 of the threshold); they act on the backing curve's low end.
- **The financed rows:** the 34 financed readings at each end or category and the markdown at 1 (48 single-reading points with the output-gap weight), and their pairs.
- **Listed as screened out** in the v5.9 preregistration, each with its largest μ* and cell (the tables' last section): the eight held Source-row readings and the six held financed readings.

## Limits

- **The box reaches regimes far from the main row.** At no assumed backing and in the financed rows, inflation runs to over 100 points a year and debt to millions of percent of GDP. There μ* measures which readings move a runaway, not which matter near the main row. The rules count those cells as the preregistration says; the full-backing view above is given for reading, not as a rule.
- **The debt outcomes are medians over seeds of exploding series.** Most points borrow nothing (the median point's debt is 0), and where they borrow the debt has no ceiling in the model (the government-budget study: whether anyone would lend is not modelled). The year debt passes GDP is counted as 21 where it never does within 20 years.
- **Twelve paths and 40 seeds rank; they do not give shares of variance.** A close call that changes a rule would call for Sobol indices; none was close enough to need one (the nearest: the bargaining pass-through at 0.98 of the threshold at full backing, carried anyway from other cells).
- **Three-level categories step from the first to the second or from the second to the third, never from the first to the third;** their μ* mixes the two steps. The one-reading choice for the tail's cutoff adds the two steps.
- **The share of points that reverse a sign** (finding 3) is a description of the paths, not a probability: Morris points are not a random sample of readings.
- **The analysis tool was extended after the results were read** (preregistration Amendment 2): its first version applied rules 1-4 only; rules 3, 5 and 6 and the secondary outcomes were added as the preregistration states them. No verdict changed between the two versions; the markdown's and the rate rule's coefficients' ratios rose once read only where they can act.
- **500 adults; 20 years;** Stress is not screened (the v5.9 preregistration carries Stress for every reading kept; every reading dropped is dropped on Reference and Adverse evidence only).
