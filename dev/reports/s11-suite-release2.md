# The visual suite on v5.9 (Nov 16, 2026)

**What changed.** The visual suite page (`suite.html`: the BLEI ladder year by year, what the days of basic living are made of, who takes part, four poverty lines side by side, and the spread across runs and environments) now opens on v5.9: no programme, the full-coverage design, built to meet NEEC's criterion C2.2, and the opt-in design, 500 paired runs, Reference, Adverse and Stress, 20 and 40 years. v5.6's page is unchanged and one click away (the "Release" switch, or `suite.html?rel=1`).

**Dated note (Nov 19, 2026): the private businesses' BU price was paid a year late.** The programme rows here paid the private businesses' lower BU price out of the previous year's premium. The design sets that price at the sale, so this was a defect, now fixed behind an option (see [the report on the private price at the sale](s90-private-price-at-sale.md)). On v5.9's main rows, setting the price at the sale makes every poverty and wealth effect slightly larger in the programme's favour and changes no sign: across both designs, three environments and 20 and 40 years, the cut in basket poverty grows by 0.04 to 0.59 points, the cut in too little wealth by 0.06 to 0.38 points and the rise in median real wealth by $110 to $1,525, and programme inflation moves by less than 0.001 points a year. These runs are v5.9's main rows recorded in more detail, so the change applies to them directly; the series on this page (the ladder's tiers, the days of basic living and their parts) were not recomputed. No rerun of this study is planned now; a rerun would be its own preregistered amendment.

**Where the numbers come from.** v5.9's stored results held yearly poverty and median wealth, but not the ladder's six tiers, the median BLEI or its parts. So v5.9's main row was run again (on GitHub Actions, free; preregistered in research-update `prereg/2026-11-x2-series-addendum.md` before it ran), recording those series. The run used today's engine (commit 5b62481) and was checked, value by value, against v5.9's stored results from engine 104691f: every engine output of every row and seed was equal (1,465,500 values per environment and horizon). So these are v5.9's runs, recorded in more detail. The results are in BetterToBest/compassionism-sim-results, folder `x2-5b62481`, with `run.txt` and `SHA256SUMS`. The page reads them from `data/releases/r2/`; v5.6's files (`data/releases/v5.6/`) are untouched.

**One more check.** Averaged over the years, the new series' share of adults under 30 days of basic living (same rules for both runs) reproduces v5.9's published basic-living figures exactly, for every row, environment and horizon.

**How to read it.** Each figure is one row's level: the mean over 500 runs, with the 10th to 90th percentile across them. The page does not subtract one row from another. Where it states a difference (under 30 days, below the cost of living, wealth poverty, inflation), it quotes v5.9's published effect with its 95% interval. v5.9's main row assumes every programme dollar is matched by new output (full backing, the design's statement; a design parameter), with today's price rule.

**What it shows (Reference, 40 years, means over the runs).** With no programme, 12.6% of adults are in Crisis in the last year and 64.7% reach Flourishing; with the full-coverage design, 0.0% and 95.8%, and the median adult covers 3,891 days of basic living against 1,537. The full-coverage design reaches every adult; under the opt-in design fewer take part, most of all in Stress, where its gains are about a third as large. Every figure is on the page with its spread, for every environment and horizon, including Adverse and Stress.

**What it does not show.** v5.9 recorded the BLEI series only: the paper's other five measures (EDC, EPPM, FBS, CSI, IPBI) and the measures study (who moves in and out of Crisis) exist for v5.6's rows only, and the v5.9 view says so. Participants and non-participants are self-selected groups. The model has not been reviewed by an independent economist.

**Correction (Nov 17, 2026, v5.9.8).** The section on what the days are made of drew the average adult's days from savings, pay and the allowance as shares of each column. The averages stored in v5.9's suite files count each of the 18 top-tail adults once instead of at its share of the population (a weight). The same fault was corrected for the wealth Gini in v5.9.5. So the average days from savings came out at 550,000 to 730,000 against a median of 1,500 to 3,800, and the chart was one colour. The section now shows the median adult's days from each part, each part on its own scale. For v5.9 it shows no averages. Weighted averages would need the main rows run again with weights in the suite export; that rerun is listed as a follow-up and has not been run.
