# Claude's reading of the design: how the Creative Collectives review work, and what happens when the review is wrong

October 5, 2026. A short note for Duke, written before building the "review errors and collusion" reading of the v5.2 round (step 7). It says what the Research Hub's papers say, what they leave open, and the cautious choice the simulation makes where they are silent. Please say if any of it misreads your design.

## What the Hub says

- **Rates rise with scrutiny.** The FAQ and the Cultural Value Integration paper set out graduated scrutiny: 1× (productive work) is automatic; 1.618× (productive and beautiful) needs peer review for the aesthetic quality; 2× to 4× (efficient, effective or inventive) needs a committee-level assessment; 5× to 6× needs an expert panel plus community input; higher rates need more again.
- **Review panels are mixed.** Peer review panels draw on domain experts and community representatives, and the evaluation is "multi-stakeholder", which the paper says prevents gaming or capture.
- **Collectives have at least 50 members**, which the Implementation Roadmap and the Glossary say "prevents gaming" and ensures "meaningful community accountability".
- **Scoring is transparent and auditable.** The Citizens Internet Portal describes "transparent, auditable scoring" of CCO merit, and the BLEI paper mentions "cross-zone audit oversight".

## What the Hub does not say

How often a review gives a rate the work does not merit, how often groups collude, and what share of such cases an audit catches. No paper gives a number for any of these.

## What the simulation does (a modelling assumption, labelled on the page)

- **The main reading is unchanged:** the review is perfect, as before.
- **Beside it, four readings:** 5%, 10% or 20% of conversions at rates above 1× are unearned (the work delivered is worth 1×; errors and collusion are counted together), and audits catch half of them, or none. A caught conversion is paid at 1×. An unearned conversion that is not caught is paid at its rate, but only its 1× value counts as new output, so the rest is new money.
- **"Audits catch half" is Claude's choice, not the Hub's.** It sits between "audits catch nothing" (shown too) and "audits catch everything" (which is the main reading). It is labelled "not specified by the Hub" wherever it appears.
- **Only the Collectives' review of project work is affected.** Business payroll in the essential-services businesses is paid by those businesses for essentials they sell, which is not the Collectives' review.

## The other readings in step 7 that the Hub does not specify

- **Paying for the Source another way (your decision C).** A progressive income tax and a land-value tax, each sized to pay for the programme in place of the Source, are marked "not specified by the Hub". The model has no land, so the land tax falls on adults in proportion to their savings; real land is owned mostly by people with more savings, so this is the closest stand-in the model allows.
- **Landlords raising rents.** The Hub's answer to this risk is community-owned housing (PTH), so PTH members' rents are never marked up in these readings.
