## 2. What Compassionism is (about 150 words)

Kept close to today's "What each design is → Compassionism" text, which Duke has already read, but shortened and with the comparison sentences removed:

- Every participant gets a monthly allowance of Basic Units (BU): a currency that only buys essentials and expires if unused.
- BU can be converted to dollars at higher rates for work the community values; a person's octave sets how much they can convert.
- Essential-service businesses accept BU, convert them, and split what they earn between lower prices, building capacity and sharing profit with their workers (the ESP split, step 1).
- Community-owned businesses and housing (PTF, PTH), zone coordination (SZH) and a civic internet portal (CIP) complete it.
- Joining is open to every adult each year (step 4).
- How it is paid for, as the Hub states it (step 3), in one sentence.

Each of these says "Claude's reading of the design" where the model fills a gap the Hub leaves open.

## 3. The live run

- One button to run a 20-year scenario in the browser (500 adults; the user can change settings).
- Beside it, the precomputed 500-seed panel (design default 7): the three environments (Reference, Adverse, Stress), each result with its interval, and the exact harness command that made it.
- Headline measure first: BLEI, days of basic living covered, as Duke's BLEI paper defines it, with the design-neutral reading beside it, labelled (design default 8; confirmed at step 8).

## 4. The results, one everyday sentence each

One sentence per result, in this order, each against no program on the same simulated people:

1. Basic living covered (BLEI).
2. How many people are poor, and how deep their poverty goes (income, basket and wealth poverty).
3. Who gains and who loses: participants, non-participants, the poorest third, the richest third.
4. Work: whether people work more or less.
5. Prices: what happens to the cost of essentials and to inflation.
6. Cost: what the program costs per adult per year, how it is paid for, and the public costs of poverty it avoids (step 9), both sides shown where the figures are contested.

No sentence claims more than the numbers show. If a result is bad, the sentence says so.
